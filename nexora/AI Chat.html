Can you explain how quantum computing differs from classical computing in simple terms?

smart_toy

Think of a classical computer like a switch. It can only be in one of two positions at a time: either ON (1) or OFF (0). Every piece of data your phone or laptop processes is built on these tiny, definite switches called bits.

A quantum computer uses "qubits." Because of a quantum rule called superposition, a qubit doesn't have to choose between being just ON or just OFF. It can be ON, OFF, or a complex combination of both at the exact same time.

Classical: 0 OR 1
Quantum: 0 AND 1 (simultaneously)
